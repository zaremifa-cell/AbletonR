## Problems Found

- The original transition was a simple opacity crossfade over a transparent frame, which let the light page surface influence the transition and created the white/light feeling the user disliked.
- The first dark revision used a blackout hold that made the transition become black too suddenly.
- The later 2.2s version had the right duration, but its brightness filters and handoff timing made the image swap too visibly detectable.
- The current and previous images were initially not rendered with identical layout mechanics: one was in normal flow and the other was an absolute overlay. The source image dimensions also differed slightly.
- Even after registering the layers, crossfading a full second product render still risked making the entire device/background feel like it was changing.
- The generated transparent overlay asset introduced a visible light/dark mismatch and was rejected by the user.
- The stable-base/overlay structure was also not the requested previous state and was reverted one step further.
- Direct crossfade overlap made pixel mismatch visible, so the final timing needed a dark hold where neither image is strongly visible.
- The first `Two ways to work` revision incorrectly placed two product images inside the right scroll pane instead of using the existing left fixed product pane for standalone.
- The next revision rendered the `Push Tethered.png` asset too small as a card-like image and placed the title inside the right scroll pane, causing the title to clip at the split boundary.
- Percentage-based height on the right visual stage allowed the media area to collapse, exposing the text grid in the first viewport of the configuration section.

## What Should Be Remembered

- The user strongly dislikes white/light flashes in this interaction.
- The user also dislikes an instant blackout; dark transitions should still feel like the accepted previous crossfade.
- For product image swaps that should feel invisible or dramatic, use a black transition surface and gradual opacity/brightness changes rather than a hard black overlay.
- Avoid brightness filter pumping when the user wants an image swap to feel seamless; it can make the transition visibly announce itself.
- For seamless image swaps, register the layers first; do not keep tuning timing while the layers use different positioning models.
- When source images are not pixel-aligned, use fade-through-dim instead of direct crossfade so the mismatch is hidden by the dark hold.
- When only one Push role image can be active, non-active controls should look disabled and be mechanically disabled until the active role is reset.
- Preserve the accepted plus/minus and role-card interaction structure while refining timing or transition feel.
- On split-screen layouts with one fixed pane and one scrolling pane, any heading intended to span both halves must live at the split parent level, not inside the scrolling pane.
- Matching bottom labels/rules across fixed and scroll panes should use one shared layout variable, not separate hand-tuned offsets.

## What Should Not Be Repeated

- Do not use a simple opacity crossfade over a light or transparent surface for dramatic Push product-image swaps.
- Do not replace a smooth accepted crossfade with an abrupt black hold.
- Do not make broad Push page restyling changes when the request only concerns this transition.
- Do not render a provided full-screen product asset as a smaller card when the user asks it to match the left product screen scale.
